Security

Vulnerability Disclosure Policy

Effective 13 October 2026 · CoreTools by JDG Initiates

  • Report security problems privately: report a vulnerability on GitHub. Please don't open a public issue.
  • We acknowledge reports within 3 business days and triage them within 10 business days.
  • Good-faith research that follows this policy is welcome, and we won't take legal action over it.
  • There is no bug bounty. We're glad to credit you in the advisory.

1. Scope

In scope

  • The CoreTools desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux, current release.
  • The in-browser app at /app and this website (coretools-chi.vercel.app).
  • The optional Google Drive backup: the sign-in flow, how the app stores your Google sign-in, and the encryption of backups.
  • Our releases: the installers, checksum files and signatures published in jaychandra1/artifacts, and how they are built and signed.

We most want to hear about anything that lets a file, web page or other program read or write data the user didn't choose; decrypt the Authenticator vault, saved logins or a cloud backup without the password; get past the vault lock or Windows Hello; send secrets off the device; run script on this site or in /app; or publish a tampered release that still passes verification.

Out of scope

  • Vulnerabilities in Google, GitHub, Vercel, Sigstore or your operating system themselves. Report those to the vendor. We do want to know if CoreTools uses them unsafely.
  • Attacks that need an already-compromised device or administrator access, unless they defeat a protection CoreTools claims to provide (vault lock, Windows Hello, OS keychain).
  • Known, documented trade-offs: installers not yet code-signed (SmartScreen or Gatekeeper warnings); the legacy encryption used only to import and export Authenticator-extension backups; account names and issuers stored unencrypted; the Google OAuth client secret inside the app (Google treats desktop-app client secrets as not confidential).
  • Denial of service, load testing, spam, social engineering and physical attacks.
  • Scanner output without a demonstrated impact, and self-XSS that needs a user to paste code into developer tools.

2. How to report

  • Preferred: GitHub private vulnerability reporting at github.com/jaychandra1/artifacts/security/advisories/new. Only the maintainers can see your report, and we can work on the fix with you there.
  • No GitHub account? Email coretools.works@gmail.com with a subject starting “Security:”. We'll reply with a private way to continue.
  • Please don't post details in a public issue, discussion or pull request, or anywhere else, until a fixed version is out.
  • Machine-readable contact details are at /.well-known/security.txt.

3. What to include

  • The CoreTools version (shown in the app's header) and your operating system, or the URL involved.
  • Which part is affected: desktop app, /app, website, Drive backup, or release files.
  • Steps to reproduce or a minimal proof of concept.
  • The impact: what an attacker gains, and what they need first.
  • A suggested fix, if you have one.
  • Whether, and under what name, you'd like to be credited.

Use test data and test accounts. Never send real secrets, yours or anyone else's.

4. What happens next

  • Within 3 business days: we acknowledge your report.
  • Within 10 business days: we triage it. We confirm whether we can reproduce it, how severe we think it is, and when we expect a fix.
  • We keep you updated while we work on it and agree a disclosure date with you.
  • The fix ships as a new release. We then publish a GitHub Security Advisory on jaychandra1/artifacts, requesting a CVE where appropriate, and credit you if you wish.
  • Please keep the issue confidential until that advisory is published, or for 90 days from your report if that comes first, unless we agree otherwise.

CoreTools is a small project. These are the targets we hold ourselves to, not a contract.

5. Safe harbor

If you make a good-faith effort to follow this policy, we will:

  • treat your research as authorized and not pursue or support legal action against you for it, including for reverse-engineering CoreTools to find the vulnerability;
  • not report you to law enforcement for it;
  • work with you to understand and fix the problem.

In return, please:

  • test only your own installations, your own accounts and your own data;
  • never access, change or delete anyone else's data, including other people's Google Drive backups. If you come across any, stop and tell us;
  • don't degrade this website or our release infrastructure (no denial of service, no high-volume automated scanning);
  • don't try to alter published releases or the jaychandra1/artifacts repository;
  • give us reasonable time to ship a fix before you disclose.

Google, GitHub and Vercel run their own services, and this policy can't authorize testing them. Follow their programs. If you're unsure whether something is allowed, ask us first.

6. No bug bounty

CoreTools is free and we don't pay rewards for reports. We'll gladly credit you in the security advisory and the release notes.

7. Verify your download

Every release in jaychandra1/artifacts includes SHA256SUMS.txt, listing the SHA-256 of every installer. Releases published since signing was introduced also include SHA256SUMS.txt.sigstore.json: a Sigstore signature tying those checksums to CoreTools' own release workflow. A file replaced by anyone else fails the check.

With cosign installed, download the installer, SHA256SUMS.txt and SHA256SUMS.txt.sigstore.json from the same release into one folder, then run: 

cosign verify-blob SHA256SUMS.txt --bundle SHA256SUMS.txt.sigstore.json \
  --certificate-identity-regexp '^https://github\.com/jaychandra1/coretools/\.github/workflows/release\.yml@refs/(tags/v.+|heads/master)$' \
  --certificate-oidc-issuer https://token.actions.githubusercontent.com
sha256sum --check --ignore-missing SHA256SUMS.txt

You should see Verified OK, then OK next to your installer. If either check fails, don't install it, and tell us.

Without cosign you can still compare checksums: on Windows certutil -hashfile CoreTools-Windows-Setup.exe SHA256, on macOS shasum -a 256 CoreTools-macOS.dmg, on Linux sha256sum CoreTools-Linux.AppImage, against the line in SHA256SUMS.txt. That proves the download matches the release page. Only the signature proves the release page itself is ours.

Published security advisories are listed at jaychandra1/artifacts → Security. To hear about new releases, watch that repository (Watch → Custom → Releases).
